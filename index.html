Anthropic Files for IPO, Setting Up One of the Most Watched Debuts in Modern Market History

By Morning Brief Staff — reporting from TechCrunch & Cointelegraph

Anthropic formally filed on Monday for an initial public offering, planning to list under the ticker “ANT” after weeks of leaked paperwork. The maker of the Claude assistant booked $16.3 billion of revenue in the first half of 2026 — up from $1.2 billion a year earlier — while narrowing its operating loss to $693 million from $2.1 billion. Its net loss nonetheless hit $80.7 billion, driven largely by non-cash accounting tied to convertible debt, and the filing warns the company “may not achieve or sustain profitability.”

The S-1 lays bare the enterprise machine behind the numbers: more than 11,900 customers spending $100,000 or more a year, 1,900 at $1 million or more, and roughly 250 at $10 million or more as of September. Governance is the fight to come — seven co-founders, through a “Founder LLC,” will hold 50.1 percent of voting power, while a Long-Term Benefit Trust appoints most of the board, a structure designed to preserve the safety mission even as public shareholders get little practical say. Cointelegraph reports the debut could come as early as November.

Microsoft Offers Buyouts to Roughly 60,000 — About a Quarter of Its Workforce

By Morning Brief Staff — reporting from Ars Technica

Microsoft said Monday it will offer voluntary buyouts to eligible employees in select parts of the company, an offer CNBC reports covers roughly 60,000 people — about a quarter of its global workforce. The voluntary exit offer extends to staff at level 70 and below in the United States, India, Canada and the United Kingdom; Chief People Officer Amy Coleman said eligible organizations were chosen “based on each business’s priorities, customer commitments and workforce needs.”

It is the company’s second voluntary reduction of 2026: an April retirement package went to 8,750 US employees — about 30 percent accepted — and the Xbox division cut 3,200 roles, some 20 percent of gaming, in July. No direct reason was given, but the move lands amid years of cost discipline while Microsoft pours money into AI infrastructure and data-center buildout.

Capital Markets · Chips

SpaceX Locks In $40 Billion GPU Lease With Apollo; Warns Rival “Neoclouds” Face a Shakeout

By Morning Brief Staff — reporting from TechCrunch

SpaceX disclosed a binding commitment Monday for roughly $40 billion in financing for AI chips and data-center equipment — which it calls the largest committed GPU financing on record. Under the deal, an Apollo-managed entity will buy the hardware, including Nvidia’s next-generation Vera Rubin chips, and lease it back for about five to five-and-a-quarter years, with closing expected by year-end and leases starting in the first half of 2027.

The scale explains the confidence: SpaceX’s AI segment — the Grok chatbot, the Cursor coding tool and its cloud compute — brought in $7.6 billion of the company’s $14.6 billion in third-quarter revenue, more than Starlink and rocket launches combined, while capital expenditures surged to nearly $20 billion. CEO Elon Musk used the announcement to warn that debt markets have shut out pure-play GPU landlords: “some will make it through, but not many.”

Security Postmortem

Cloudflare Details the GLM-5.3 Bug That Let Strangers’ Prompts Bleed Into 155 Responses

By Morning Brief Staff — from the Cloudflare blog

Cloudflare published a coordinated disclosure with the SGLang project on a cross-request KV-cache exposure in the GLM-5.3 model on its Workers AI platform. Between August 27 and September 9, a small number of customers sending very long prompts — all longer than 681,280 tokens — received responses containing fragments of other users’ sessions: 155 responses in total. The cause was a mis-sized cache pool colliding with a SGLang bug in disaggregated prefill/decode mode; after researcher Gadi Evron of Knostic reported it via HackerOne on September 8, Cloudflare pulled the model within 40 minutes and restored it the next day. The company found no evidence of deliberate exploitation.

Big Cash Deals Face the Squeeze: Bank of Thailand Orders Checks on B5m-Plus Days From Nov 1

By Morning Brief Staff — from Bangkok Post (pub. Oct 26, 14:50 ICT)

From November 1, financial institutions must apply enhanced due diligence to same-day cash transactions of 5 million baht or more at physical branches, the Bank of Thailand ordered Monday. The rules cover cash deposits, Thai banknote exchanges, purchases of cheques or drafts, foreign-exchange deals, insurance payments, investment products and derivatives — customers must show the source and purpose of the money, and institutions must report anything suspicious to the central bank. Electronic transactions are exempt from the requirement.
